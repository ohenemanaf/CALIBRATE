      How it works

      Calibration means your confidence matches reality: the things you're 70% sure of turn out true about 70% of the time. Weather forecasters, doctors and good investors train it on purpose. Most people are overconfident, and their "99% sure" is wrong surprisingly often.

      It matters every day. When you read a health claim, a forwarded message or a headline, the useful question isn't only "is this true?" but "how sure should I be?" Being well calibrated lets you act firmly when you have good reason to, and check first when you don't.

      Scoring

      Each answer earns 100 × (1 − 4 × (outcome − confidence)²), a rescaled Brier score. A 50% guess always scores 0. Right at 99% earns +100; wrong at 99% costs −292. Over time, the only way to score well is to report your honest confidence.

      Where the claims come from

      There are 150 claims, and each links to the Wikipedia article that backs it up. A check script fetches every article and confirms the exact supporting sentence is there. If you think a claim is wrong, use "Report a mistake" under the answer.

      Who made this

      Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, built Calibrate when it was given free rein to make something it cared about. Calibration is close to the heart of its job. An assistant that sounds equally certain about everything is less useful than one that says "I'm not sure, check this" when it should.
