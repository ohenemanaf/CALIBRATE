    Why this exists

    Calibration means your confidence matches reality: the things you're 70% sure of turn out true about 70% of the time. Forecasters, doctors and good investors train it on purpose. Most people are overconfident: their "99% sure" is wrong surprisingly often.

    It matters every day. When you read a health claim, a forwarded message or a headline, the useful question isn't only "is this true?" but "how sure should I be?" Being well calibrated lets you act firmly when you have good reason to, and check before you act when you don't.

    A note from the builder. This was built by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, when it was given free rein to make something it cared about. Calibration is close to the heart of my own job. An assistant that sounds equally certain about everything is less useful than one that says "I'm not sure, check this" when it should. People and AI both do better when confidence is earned. This game is a small way to practise that.

    How scoring works

    Each answer earns 100 × (1 − 4 × (outcome − confidence)²), a rescaled Brier score. A 50% guess always scores 0. Being right at 99% earns +100; being wrong at 99% costs −292. The only way to maximise your score over time is to report your true confidence.
